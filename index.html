Rent vs Delayed Buy — Optimal Home Purchase Timing Calculator

Is it better to keep renting, or to buy? And if buying, should you buy now or wait and save a larger down payment first?

Both questions come down to your total wealth at the sale month. Both paths get the same monthly budget, and each pays its own housing costs out of it.

Everything runs in your browser, and anything you save stays on this device. Your data is never sent anywhere.

How the timeline works
Today → Month B: you rent, and invest your cash on hand plus whatever income the rent leaves. This becomes the down payment.
Month B: you buy at the price the house has grown to, spending the down payment and the closing costs, and take on the mortgage.
Month B → Month S: you own the home, paying the mortgage and the running costs out of income, then sell at Month S.
Compared against: never buying at all — renting for the whole S months and investing throughout. Both paths are totted up at Month S, which is what makes them comparable.
What the tool tells you: whether you should buy at all — and if you should, the best time to buy. On the chart, buying wins wherever the delayed buy path sits above the flat rent line; the month at its highest point is the best Month B to buy.

Inputs

Rental Assumptions

These assumptions estimate the cost of renting while you wait to buy. They are used to compare the buy-and-sell path against continuing to rent through Month S.

Home Purchase Assumptions

These inputs define the target home and the financing terms. The stated price is treated as today's value, then the calculator grows it over time until Month B and again until Month S.

Mortgage Default Insurance

If your down payment is below 20%, most lenders require mortgage default insurance. Choose how you want the premium to be handled. If you leave the custom rate at 0%, the calculator uses a conservative default band based on your down-payment percentage.

CMHC rates used when custom = 0: DP <5% → 4.00% (not insurable in practice) | DP 5–9.99% → 4.00% | DP 10–14.99% → 3.10% | DP 15–19.99% → 2.80% | DP ≥20% → 0%. Ontario also charges 8% PST on the premium at closing, which this calculator does not include.

Waiting Period Investment Assumptions

This is the money that keeps accumulating until Month B and continues compounding through Month B. It becomes the down payment pool used for the purchase at Month B.

Post-Purchase Assumptions

These inputs cover the recurring costs of owning the property and the one-time costs of selling it. They reduce the final wealth you keep after the buy-and-sell path. Note: renter insurance is modeled as a fixed annual premium (with its own annual increase), while homeowner insurance is modeled as a percentage of home value (replacement-cost aligned).
